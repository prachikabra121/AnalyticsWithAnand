HUMAN RESOURCES POLICIES

Purpose and Scope
These policies describe general workplace expectations for employees and managers. They are intended as a practical guide and should be read together with employment agreements, benefit documents, and applicable law. They should be adapted to the organization, its location, and its workforce. Where a policy conflicts with applicable law, the law takes precedence. HR is responsible for maintaining the current version and communicating material updates.

1. Equal Opportunity, Inclusion, and Respect
The organization provides equal employment opportunity. Hiring, pay, promotion, training, discipline, and other employment decisions should be based on role requirements, qualifications, performance, and business needs. Discrimination, harassment, bullying, and retaliation are prohibited.

Employees should use respectful language, respect reasonable personal and cultural boundaries, and avoid conduct that creates an intimidating, hostile, or humiliating environment. Harassment can include unwelcome verbal, physical, written, or online conduct. Employees who experience or witness inappropriate conduct should report it to a manager, HR, or another designated contact. Reports will be reviewed fairly and promptly, with information shared only as needed to address the concern. Retaliation for a good-faith report or participation in a review is prohibited.

The organization will consider reasonable workplace accommodations as required by law. Employees seeking an accommodation should contact HR or their manager and provide information needed to discuss an effective arrangement.

2. Recruitment, Hiring, and Onboarding
Hiring decisions should use job-related criteria and a consistent process. Applicants should be treated fairly, and interview or assessment information should be handled confidentially. Any required background or reference checks must be conducted lawfully and with appropriate notice or consent.

New employees should receive information about their role, reporting line, work schedule, pay and benefits, workplace policies, safety procedures, and the tools needed for their work. Required onboarding and compliance training should be completed within the communicated timeframe.

3. Professional Conduct and Conflicts of Interest
Employees are expected to act honestly, perform their duties with care, follow reasonable workplace instructions, and treat colleagues, customers, suppliers, and visitors professionally. Employees must not falsify records, misuse their role, or engage in fraud, threats, or violence.

Employees should disclose situations where personal, family, or financial interests could affect—or appear to affect—their work decisions. Examples can include selecting a vendor in which an employee has an interest, supervising a close relative, or accepting a substantial gift from a business partner. HR or management will review disclosures and determine reasonable steps to manage the conflict. Gifts and hospitality must follow company rules and applicable law.

4. Working Hours, Attendance, and Remote Work
Employees should follow their agreed schedule, accurately record hours or attendance where required, and obtain approval for overtime or schedule changes when the process requires it. Employees should notify their manager as soon as practical about lateness, absence, or an unexpected interruption to work. Managers should apply attendance expectations consistently and handle sensitive absence information discreetly.

Remote or hybrid work requires manager approval and may depend on role duties, performance, coverage, and information security. Employees working remotely should be reachable during agreed work hours, protect company information from being viewed or accessed by others, use approved devices and systems, and maintain a safe work area. Any changes to an approved arrangement should be discussed with the manager. These guidelines do not change statutory rights or contractual terms.

5. Pay, Benefits, and Records
Pay, deductions, benefits, and payroll schedules are governed by the employee's agreement, plan documents, and applicable law. Employees should review their pay statements and promptly report suspected errors to HR or payroll. The organization will maintain personnel and payroll records with appropriate access controls and retention practices. Employees should keep their contact, emergency contact, tax, and payment details current through the approved process.

6. Leave and Time Off

6.1 Sample Policy Notice
The leave balances and rules in this section are fictional sample values for demonstration. They are not a statement of legal entitlement. Before adopting this policy, HR must check local law, employment agreements, and applicable benefit plans. Where a law or agreement provides a greater entitlement, that entitlement applies. Unless stated otherwise, one leave day means one scheduled workday for the employee.

6.2 Leave Year and Annual Allowance
The sample leave year runs from 1 January through 31 December. A full-time employee receives the following paid leave allowance in each complete leave year:
- Annual leave (vacation/earned leave): 18 working days per year, accrued at 1.5 days per completed month.
- Sick leave: 10 working days per year, credited as 10 days on 1 January.
- Casual/personal leave: 6 working days per year, credited as 6 days on 1 January.
- Total regular paid leave: up to 34 days per full year, excluding public holidays, statutory leave, and approved compensatory time off.

Part-time employees receive leave on a pro-rated basis according to their scheduled working days or hours. Employees joining or leaving during the leave year receive a pro-rated annual leave allowance based on completed service; HR will calculate the balance and explain any rounding. New employees accrue annual leave from their start date. For this sample policy, leave may be used after 30 calendar days of employment, subject to accrued balance and manager approval. No employee may use a negative balance unless HR approves an exception in writing.

6.3 Annual Leave (Vacation/Earned Leave)
Annual leave is intended for rest, vacation, and planned personal needs. It accrues monthly at 1.5 working days, up to 18 days in a complete calendar year. Employees should submit requests at least 10 calendar days before a planned absence of one or two days and at least 30 calendar days before an absence of three or more consecutive workdays. Managers should consider staffing, fairness across the team, and the order in which requests were received, and respond within five working days where practicable. Approval should be recorded in the leave system or another designated written record before travel is booked.

A maximum of 8 unused annual leave days may be carried into the next leave year. Carried days must be used by 31 March of that next year; any remaining carried days expire after that date unless local law requires a longer period or HR approves an extension. Annual leave above the 8-day carry-forward limit does not roll over under this sample policy. Annual leave is not automatically paid out each year. On separation, any payout or forfeiture is handled according to applicable law and the employee's agreement.

6.4 Sick Leave
Sick leave is for an employee's illness, injury, medical appointment, or care needs where permitted by applicable law. Employees should notify their manager before their scheduled start time, or as soon as reasonably possible during an emergency, and provide an estimated return date if known. Sick leave may be taken in half-day or full-day increments. For an absence lasting more than three consecutive scheduled workdays, HR may request reasonable supporting documentation, subject to privacy law. Employees should not send detailed diagnoses to their manager; medical information should be sent only to HR or the designated confidential channel.

The sample allowance is 10 working days per leave year. Unused sick leave does not carry forward and is not paid out, except where law or an applicable plan requires otherwise. If the allowance is exhausted, employees may request available annual leave, another legally protected leave, or unpaid leave. The organization will consider accommodation requests and protected leave rights as required by law.

6.5 Casual/Personal Leave
Casual leave is for short-notice personal matters that cannot reasonably be scheduled outside work. The sample allowance is 6 working days per leave year. Employees should give at least one working day's notice where possible; for an urgent matter, notify the manager as soon as practical. Casual leave may be taken in half-day or full-day increments, but no more than two consecutive working days should be taken without manager and HR review. Unused casual leave does not carry forward and is not paid out, except where law requires otherwise. Planned vacations should be charged to annual leave rather than casual leave.

6.6 Public Holidays and Weekly Rest Days
The organization publishes a holiday calendar each year. Designated public holidays and the employee's regular weekly rest days are not deducted from annual leave when they fall during an approved leave period. If business needs require an employee to work on a public holiday or rest day, the manager must arrange compensation or substitute time off in accordance with applicable law and company procedures.

6.7 Parental, Maternity, Paternity, Adoption, and Caregiver Leave
Statutory parental, maternity, paternity, adoption, and caregiver leave is provided in accordance with applicable law and benefit plans. Employees should contact HR as early as practical to understand eligibility, required notices, documentation, pay treatment, and return-to-work arrangements. Medical and family information will be handled confidentially.

For this fictional example only, a non-birthing parent may receive 10 paid working days for the birth or adoption of a child, to be used within 90 days of the event. This sample benefit does not reduce any longer or more generous statutory or contractual entitlement. Any additional time may be requested as annual leave, another applicable leave, or unpaid leave, subject to law and approval.

6.8 Bereavement and Compassionate Leave
For this sample policy, employees may take up to 3 paid working days following the death of an immediate family member (spouse or partner, child, parent, sibling, or person who lives in the employee's household). HR may approve additional time, annual leave, or unpaid leave based on the circumstances. Employees should notify their manager as soon as practical. Documentation should be requested only where reasonably necessary and permitted by law.

6.9 Unpaid Leave and Extended Absence
Employees who need time away after paid balances are exhausted may submit a written request for unpaid leave. The request should state the dates, reason category, and expected return date without including unnecessary sensitive details. HR and the manager will review business coverage, applicable protections, benefit effects, and return-to-work arrangements. Approval must be documented. Legally protected leave will be handled under applicable law and will not be denied based only on this discretionary process.

6.10 Compensatory Time Off
Overtime or work on a rest day does not automatically create compensatory time off. Any time-off-in-lieu arrangement must be approved in advance, recorded accurately, and administered according to applicable law. For this fictional sample, approved compensatory time should be used within 60 calendar days. HR will confirm any different legal or contractual requirement.

6.11 Request, Approval, and Handover Process
1. Submit the request through the designated HR or leave system, selecting the leave type and exact dates or hours.
2. The system or HR will show the current balance; employees should check it before submitting. A request is not approved until the manager or authorized approver records approval.
3. Before planned leave, employees should document urgent work, hand over time-sensitive tasks, and agree on a contact for genuine emergencies. Managers should not require employees on leave to remain routinely available.
4. For an unexpected absence, notify the manager promptly using the agreed channel. Submit or correct the leave entry when able, normally within two working days after returning.
5. Managers should review requests consistently and respond promptly. If a request cannot be approved for coverage reasons, the manager should explain the reason and, where practical, suggest alternative dates.
6. HR maintains the official leave balance and resolves discrepancies. Employees should report an incorrect balance promptly.

6.12 Examples of Balance and Carry-Forward
Example A: An employee takes 12 annual leave days during the year and has accrued all 18 days. The remaining 6 annual leave days may be carried into the next year, subject to the 31 March use-by date.

Example B: An employee has 12 unused annual leave days on 31 December. Under this sample rule, 8 days carry forward and must be used by 31 March; the other 4 days expire unless law or an approved exception provides otherwise.

Example C: An employee has 4 unused sick days and 2 unused casual days on 31 December. These balances do not carry forward under this sample policy. HR must apply any mandatory legal carry-forward or payout rule instead.

Example D: An employee joins on 1 July. The annual leave allowance is calculated pro rata for the remaining service period; HR confirms the exact accrued balance in the leave system. Sick and casual leave are also pro-rated for this sample policy, subject to any greater legal entitlement.

6.13 Leave During Notice and Separation
Leave during a notice period must be requested and approved through the usual process, subject to applicable law and the employee's agreement. Employees should not assume that unused leave automatically extends a notice period. At separation, HR will calculate remaining balances and any required payment or adjustment under applicable law and the employee's agreement. The organization will not deduct amounts from final pay unless the deduction is lawful and properly authorized.
7. Health, Safety, and Wellbeing
Everyone shares responsibility for a safe workplace. Employees should follow safety instructions, use required protective equipment, keep work areas orderly, and report hazards, injuries, illness related to work, and near misses promptly to a manager or the designated safety contact. Employees should cooperate with safety training and emergency procedures.

Threats, violence, and working while impaired in a way that creates a safety risk are not permitted. Employees should seek help if they believe there is an immediate danger and use the organization's emergency reporting process. The organization will handle safety reports promptly and will not retaliate against good-faith reporting.

8. Confidentiality, Privacy, and Data Protection
Employees must protect confidential business, personnel, and customer information. Access information only when needed for work, use approved systems to store or share it, verify recipients before sending sensitive material, and follow retention and disposal rules. Do not discuss confidential information in public places or with people who are not authorized to receive it.

Suspected loss, mistaken disclosure, phishing, or unauthorized access should be reported promptly to the designated IT, security, privacy, or management contact. Employees must handle personal information in line with company procedures and applicable privacy law. Confidentiality obligations may continue after employment ends, subject to applicable law.

9. Company Equipment and Technology
Company devices, accounts, networks, and software should be used responsibly and primarily for authorized business purposes. Employees must secure devices, use approved authentication methods, protect passwords and access tokens, and promptly report lost equipment or suspected account compromise. Do not install unapproved software, bypass security controls, or share credentials.

Any personal use permitted by company rules must be limited, lawful, and must not interfere with work or compromise security. Use of company systems may be monitored only as permitted by law and company policy, with appropriate notice where required.

10. Performance, Training, and Development
Managers should communicate role expectations, provide regular feedback, recognize good work, and address performance concerns promptly and fairly. Employees should ask for clarification when expectations are unclear, participate in check-ins and reviews, and complete required training by the stated deadlines.

When performance needs improvement, managers should explain the concern, the expected standard, available support, and a reasonable review period where appropriate. Development opportunities may depend on role needs, eligibility, and available resources.

11. Grievances, Investigations, and Reporting Concerns
Employees may raise workplace concerns with their manager, HR, or another designated contact. If the concern involves that person, employees may use another available reporting channel. Reports should include relevant dates, events, and people involved when possible; employees are not expected to investigate matters themselves.

The organization will assess reports, decide whether further review is needed, and take appropriate action. Employees are expected to cooperate honestly with a review and preserve relevant records. Information will be handled as confidentially as practical, but complete confidentiality cannot be guaranteed when a fair investigation or legal obligation requires disclosure. Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith or assists a review is prohibited.

12. Corrective Action and Separation
Policy violations may result in coaching or corrective action, up to and including termination, consistent with applicable law, employment agreements, and company procedures. The organization will consider the circumstances and available information before deciding on action. Nothing in this policy changes any rights or obligations established by law or contract.

When employment ends, employees should return company property, transfer work materials as instructed, submit outstanding time or expense records, and follow applicable confidentiality and data-handling requirements. HR will provide information about final pay, benefits, and required documents in accordance with applicable law.

13. Questions, Exceptions, and Updates
For questions, accommodation requests, or policy guidance, contact HR or the designated manager. Any exception must be approved by the appropriate authorized person and must comply with applicable law. The organization may revise these policies and will communicate material changes. Employees are responsible for reviewing updates and completing any required acknowledgment.
