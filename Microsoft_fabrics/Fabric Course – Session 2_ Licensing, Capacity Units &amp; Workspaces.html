1. Two things you must license

New learners almost always mix these up. Fabric licensing actually has two independent parts, and every person and every workload needs both pieces sorted out.

⚙️ Capacity licensing
Answers: who pays for the compute that runs the workloads?
Trial capacity, F-SKU (F2 to F2048), or Power BI Premium capacity.
👤 Per-user licensing
Answers: who is allowed to author or view Power BI content?
Free, Power BI Pro, or Premium Per User (PPU).
💡 Analogy: Capacity is like the electricity connection to a building — it powers everything inside. Per-user licenses are like the keycards that decide who is allowed to enter which rooms. You need both: power for the building, and the right keycard for each person.

A workspace is always assigned to one capacity. The people working in that workspace each need the right per-user license depending on whether they create or only view content, and depending on the size of the capacity (see the F64 threshold, Section 6).

3. Capacity Units (CUs) and the F-SKU family

A Capacity Unit (CU) is Fabric's single unit of compute. Every workload — a Spark job, a pipeline run, a warehouse query, a Power BI refresh, even opening a report — draws CUs from the same shared pool. This is different from the old world, where Spark, the warehouse and Power BI each had their own separate compute to size and pay for.

Capacities are named by their CU count and the family doubles at every step:

4. Pricing models

ModelHow it worksBest for
Pay-as-you-go (PAYG)Billed per hour while the capacity is running. You can pause it any time and stop paying for compute.Dev/test, learning, spiky or unpredictable workloads
ReservedCommit to a capacity for a term (e.g. 1 or 3 years) in exchange for a significant discount versus PAYG.Steady, always-on production workloads
💰 Cost tip: pausing a capacity stops the compute charge immediately. Your workspaces, items and data are untouched while paused. Pausing non-production capacities overnight or on weekends is one of the simplest ways to control cost.
📌 Always confirm current rates on Microsoft's official Fabric pricing page before quoting numbers to a client — prices and regions vary and change over time.

5. Bursting, smoothing and throttling

These three ideas explain what actually happens when a workload asks for more compute than your capacity size provides.

⚡ Bursting
A job is temporarily allowed to use more CUs than your SKU size so it can finish faster, instead of being immediately blocked.
🌊 Smoothing
That extra "borrowed" usage isn't charged all at once. It is spread out over a rolling time window, so a short spike doesn't look like a big overload.
🚦 Throttling
If usage stays above your capacity for too long, Fabric starts to reject new requests, first with a temporary rejection, and then more strictly if it continues.
📱 Analogy: Think of a mobile data plan with a small grace buffer. If you go over your limit for a few minutes, nothing happens — the network smooths it out. If you keep streaming HD video non-stop past your limit for hours, your speed gets throttled until you recover.
Normal use
Usage stays within CU limit
Short burst
Briefly over limit, smoothed over time — no visible impact
Sustained overuse
Consistently over limit — requests get delayed, then rejected

6. The F64 threshold

  • F64 and above: users with only a Free license can view Power BI content shared through that capacity. Only the people creating content need Pro.
  • Below F64 (F2 through F32): every viewer also needs a Pro (or PPU) license, in addition to the capacity itself.

This single threshold is why F64 is a well-known landmark number in Fabric licensing conversations.

Worked example (validate against current pricing before using with a client):
A company has 5 report developers and 200 report viewers.

Option A — small capacity + Pro for everyone: 200 viewers × a Pro license each, roughly $12–14/user/month, is roughly $2,500–2,800/month, plus the cost of a small F-SKU running only for working hours.

Option B — F64 capacity: the 200 viewers need no Pro license at all (Free is enough), but F64 itself costs roughly several thousand dollars a month if it runs continuously on pay-as-you-go — commonly cited estimates are in the $8,000–9,000/month range, though this varies by region and changes over time.

Take-away: for a smaller number of viewers, a small SKU plus Pro licenses is usually cheaper. As viewer count grows into the low hundreds, F64 starts to look competitive because the per-viewer Pro cost disappears. The exact break-even point depends on current prices — this is exactly what the assignment asks you to calculate.

7. Workspace fundamentals

  • A workspace is a container for Fabric items: Lakehouse, Warehouse, pipelines, notebooks, reports, and more.
  • Each workspace is assigned to exactly one capacity at a time.
  • Workspace roles — Admin, Member, Contributor, Viewer — control what people can do inside it. These are covered in full in Session 4 (Security).
  • Use clear naming that shows environment and purpose, for example Retail-Dev and Retail-Prod, so teams instantly know what a workspace is for.
  • A workspace can have a description and a contact list, which help other teams know who owns it and who to contact for support.

🧪 Lab 2: Capacity Metrics app and workspaces

Time: 20 minutes · Needs: your trial capacity from Session 1, and capacity admin rights.

  1. Go to AppSource (from the Fabric portal, or directly) and install the Microsoft Fabric Capacity Metrics app. Expected: the app appears in your list of installed Power BI apps.
  2. Open the app and connect it to your trial (or F-SKU) capacity. Expected: a dashboard loads showing capacity utilization over time.
  3. Go to Workspaces → New workspace and create Retail-Dev.
  4. Repeat and create a second workspace, Retail-Prod.
  5. For each workspace, open Settings → License info and assign it to your trial capacity.
  6. For each workspace, open Settings → General and fill in a short description and a contact list (your own name/email is fine for now).
  7. Do something small in one workspace (e.g. create a Lakehouse) to generate a little activity.
  8. Return to the Capacity Metrics app after a few minutes and locate your workspace's utilization in the dashboard.

✅ Checkpoint: both workspaces show the capacity assignment, have a description and contact list filled in, and the Metrics app shows at least some data for your capacity. Take a screenshot of the Metrics app dashboard for your assignment.

🛠 Common problems and fixes
  • Can't install the Metrics app: you likely need capacity admin rights, not just workspace admin rights. Ask your Fabric administrator.
  • Metrics app shows no data yet: metrics can take a few minutes to appear after activity. Do a small action in a workspace and check again.
  • Can't assign a workspace to capacity: you need to be a workspace admin, and you need permission to assign that specific capacity.
  • Trial capacity has disappeared: trials last 60 days; check the Admin Portal's Capacity settings for remaining time.

📝 Self-check quiz

1. What is the difference between capacity licensing and per-user licensing?Capacity licensing pays for the shared compute that runs every Fabric workload. Per-user licensing controls who is allowed to author or consume Power BI content.
2. How many CUs does an F32 provide, and what is the next size up?32 CUs. The next size up is F64 (64 CUs).
3. Explain smoothing in one sentence.Smoothing spreads a workload's extra compute usage over a rolling time window, so a short spike doesn't immediately cause throttling.
4. What happens if a capacity is sustained well above its CU limit for a long time?Fabric progressively throttles it: new requests get delayed, and if the overuse continues, requests are increasingly rejected until usage falls back within limits.
5. Why does F64 matter for Power BI viewers specifically?On F64 and above, viewers need only a Free license to view shared content. Below F64, every viewer also needs a Pro or PPU license.
6. Name one way to reduce the cost of a non-production capacity.Use pay-as-you-go pricing and pause the capacity when it isn't in use, or choose the smallest SKU size that comfortably fits the workload.
7. Can one workspace be split across two capacities?No. A workspace is always assigned to exactly one capacity at a time.
8. Interview style: "How would you decide between F32 and F64 for a client?"Model answer: look at the number of Power BI viewers, workload volume (pipelines, Spark, warehouse), and growth plans. Calculate the F64-without-Pro cost against the smaller-SKU-plus-Pro-licenses cost using current pricing, and factor in headroom for future workloads before recommending one.
